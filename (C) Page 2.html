ISRO

INDIAN SPACE RESEARCH ORGANIZATION

Our Proud History

From carrying rocket parts on a bicycle to landing on the Moon's South Pole, ISRO has defined India's technological strength. Founded in 1969 by Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, it continues to make space accessible for everyone.

Recent major successes include Chandrayaan-3, which made India the first nation to reach the lunar south pole region, and the Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan).

James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)

Launched in December 2021, JWST is the most powerful space telescope ever built. It orbits 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, using infrared vision to look back over 13.5 billion years to see the first stars and galaxies forming in the early universe.

Phenomenas

Supernova

The explosive end of a star's life.

A supernova is the colossal, violent explosion of a star at the end of its life, outshining an entire galaxy for weeks. It occurs in two main ways: a massive star collapses under its own gravity after running out of fuel, or a white dwarf steals too much mass from a companion star, triggering runaway nuclear fusion. These energetic outbursts blast stellar material into space, creating heavy elements vital for new star and planet formation. The core remaining behind becomes a dense neutron star or a black hole. They are rare, yet powerful cosmic events.

Black Hole

A point of infinite density and gravity.

Black holes are among the most mysterious cosmic objects, much studied but not fully understood. These objects aren’t really holes. They’re huge concentrations of matter packed into very tiny spaces. A black hole is so dense that gravity just beneath its surface, the event horizon, is strong enough that nothing – not even light – can escape. The event horizon isn’t a surface like Earth’s or even the Sun’s. It’s a boundary that contains all the matter that makes up the black hole. There is much we don’t know about black holes, like what matter looks like inside their event horizons. However, there is a lot that scientists do know about black holes

Nebula

Vast clouds where stars are born.

nebula is a vast, interstellar cloud of dust, hydrogen, helium, and other ionized gases, often acting as a nursery for new stars. Ranging in size from hundreds of light-years across to entirely surrounding dying stars, they are formed either from the remnants of stellar explosions (supernovae) or as regions of cold gas clumping together to start star formation
